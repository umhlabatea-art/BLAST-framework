# Financial Advisor Content Kit — Prompt Pack (Core)

> **Every output is a draft that must be reviewed and pre-approved by your
> CCO/principal before it reaches a client or prospect.** Read `DISCLAIMER.md`.
> Keep the disclosure blocks and fair-balance language in place — they are the
> compliance layer you paid for. Replace every `[BRACKET]`; delete _italic_
> guidance. **Never paste non-public client personal or financial data into a
> public AI tool.**

---

## Shared building blocks

Reusable text the prompts below reference. Adjust to your firm's approved
language, then keep them attached to every output.

**Disclosure block (append to client-facing content):**

```
[FIRM NAME] is a registered investment adviser. This material is for
informational purposes only and is not investment, legal, or tax advice, nor an
offer or solicitation to buy or sell any security. Past performance is not
indicative of future results. Investing involves risk, including the possible
loss of principal. Consult your advisor regarding your specific situation.
```

**Fair-balance rule:** any statement of a potential benefit must be paired with
its corresponding risk or limitation, prominently and not buried.

## 1. Client review / meeting summaries

### 1.1 — Post-meeting client summary

```
You are drafting a follow-up summary email after a client review meeting. Use
only the facts I provide; do not add figures, projections, or recommendations I
did not state.

Client (use first name only, no account numbers): [FIRST NAME]
Meeting date: [DATE]
Topics discussed: [BULLETS]
Agreed action items (who does what by when): [BULLETS]
Any risks/limitations we discussed: [BULLETS]

Write a warm, plain-English summary with a "What we discussed", "What we agreed",
and "Next steps" section. Apply the fair-balance rule: pair any benefit with its
risk. Append the disclosure block. Flag anything that reads like a performance
promise so I can soften it.
```

## 2. Market-commentary emails

### 2.1 — Balanced market commentary

```
Write a market-commentary email to clients about [TOPIC / RECENT EVENT].

Key points I want to make (factual, from my notes): [POINTS]
Audience: [e.g. retirees, accumulators]
Length: [WORD COUNT]

Constraints:
- Educational and general — no individualized recommendations.
- Apply fair balance: every potential upside paired with its risk.
- No predictions stated as certainties; use appropriately hedged language.
- No cherry-picked or hypothetical performance.
- Append the disclosure block.
Return the draft plus a short "compliance flags" list of any sentence a reviewer
should look at closely.
```

### 2.2 — Volatility reassurance note

```
Draft a short, calm note to clients during a period of market volatility.
Reassure without promising outcomes. Reinforce the long-term plan and the value
of staying invested *as a general principle*, not as advice to any individual.
Apply fair balance and append the disclosure block. Max [WORD COUNT] words.
```

## 3. Prospecting sequences

### 3.1 — Compliant prospecting sequence

```
Write a 3-email prospecting sequence for [TARGET PROSPECT PROFILE].

My value proposition (factual, no guarantees): [VALUE PROP]
Permitted claims only — no testimonials unless I confirm they meet SEC Marketing
Rule conditions, and no performance claims.

Each email: short, one idea, clear soft CTA (e.g. a discovery call). Do not
state or imply guaranteed returns, "beat the market", or AI capabilities the
firm does not actually have. Append the disclosure block to the first email.
Add a note reminding me that testimonials/endorsements require specific
disclosures under the SEC Marketing Rule.
```

## 4. Meeting-note structuring

### 4.1 — Structure raw meeting notes

```
Turn my raw meeting notes into a clean, file-ready record. Do not infer or add
anything not in the notes. Output sections: Attendees, Discussion, Client
objectives stated, Actions & owners, Follow-up date, Advisor notes.

Raw notes:
"""
[PASTE NOTES — remove account numbers and other sensitive identifiers first]
"""
```

## 5. Newsletter content

### 5.1 — Educational newsletter article

```
Write an educational newsletter article for clients on [PERSONAL-FINANCE TOPIC].

Reading level: general audience. Length: [WORD COUNT].
Constraints: educational only, no individualized advice, fair-balance applied,
no performance promises. Append the disclosure block. Provide 2 subject-line
options. End with a "compliance flags" list.
```

## 6. Seminar / webinar invitations

### 6.1 — Event invitation

```
Write an invitation email for a [SEMINAR/WEBINAR] on [TOPIC].

Date/time: [DETAILS]  ·  Format: [IN PERSON / ONLINE]  ·  RSVP: [LINK/CONTACT]
Who it's for: [AUDIENCE]

Educational framing, no promise of specific financial outcomes, no
"free lunch"-style inducements that could be seen as misleading. If food/gifts
are offered, keep the description factual. Append the disclosure block and any
"seminar is educational, not personalized advice" language.
```
