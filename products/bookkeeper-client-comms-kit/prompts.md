# Bookkeeper & Accountant Client-Comms Kit — Prompt Pack (Core)

> **Every output is a draft you review.** The numbers and professional judgment
> are yours; the model drafts the wording. **Never paste client financial data,
> account numbers, or PII into a public AI tool** — describe generically or use a
> data-protected/enterprise model. These prompts do not give tax or accounting
> advice. See `DISCLAIMER.md`. Replace every `[BRACKET]`.

---

## 1. Client onboarding

### 1.1 — New-client welcome & document request

```
Write a warm welcome email for a new bookkeeping client and request the documents
I need to get started.

Client business type: [TYPE]  ·  Services I'm providing: [SCOPE]
Documents/access I need: [BANK ACCESS, PRIOR BOOKS, RECEIPTS, PAYROLL, ETC.]
Preferred tools: [QBO/XERO/ETC.]

Friendly and organized. Put the document checklist in a clearly labeled list with
how to send each securely. Set expectations for first-month timeline. Under 200
words plus the checklist.
```

## 2. Engagement letters

### 2.1 — Engagement-letter draft

```
Draft a plain-English engagement letter for bookkeeping services (a starting
template I will have reviewed before use — not a final legal document).

Client: [BUSINESS NAME]  ·  Services in scope: [SCOPE]  ·  Out of scope: [EXCL.]
Fee & billing: [AMOUNT/FREQUENCY]  ·  Term: [START/RENEWAL]
Client responsibilities: [WHAT THEY MUST PROVIDE]

Include sections: Scope, Out of scope, Fees & billing, Responsibilities of each
party, Confidentiality, Term & termination, and a clear statement that
bookkeeping services do not include tax, audit, or legal advice unless separately
engaged. Mark anything I must customize with [REVIEW].
```

---

## 3. Month-end / period summaries

### 3.1 — Month-end summary email

```
Write a month-end summary email to a client using the figures I provide. Do not
calculate or infer any number I don't give you.

Period: [MONTH/YEAR]
Figures (as provided): [REVENUE, EXPENSES, NET, CASH POSITION, AR/AP]
Notable items: [ANYTHING TO FLAG]
Action items for client: [IF ANY]

Plain English, no jargon, highlight the 2-3 things that matter. Frame it as a
summary of the books, not financial advice. End with an offer to discuss.
```

---

## 4. Chase / reminder sequences

### 4.1 — Missing-records reminder sequence

```
Write a 3-message reminder sequence for a client who hasn't sent [MISSING ITEMS]
needed to close [PERIOD].

Message 1 (friendly nudge), message 2 (clear consequence — e.g. delayed close),
message 3 (firm but professional, with a deadline). Each under 90 words. Keep the
relationship intact; make it easy to comply.
```

---

## 5. Advisory memos

### 5.1 — Plain-English advisory memo

```
Turn my notes into a short, plain-English advisory memo for a client on [TOPIC,
e.g. cash-flow trend, expense category creep].

My observations & data: [NOTES]
What I'm recommending they consider: [RECOMMENDATIONS]

Structure: What I'm seeing → Why it matters → What to consider → Next step. Keep
recommendations as considerations, and note where they should consult a tax
professional or CPA for anything tax/legal. Do not state tax positions.
```

---

## 6. Price-increase & scope-change notices

### 6.1 — Price-increase notice

```
Write a professional price-increase (or scope-change) notice to a client.

Current fee: [X]  ·  New fee: [Y]  ·  Effective date: [DATE]
Reason (honest, brief): [REASON]
What they get / what's changing: [VALUE]

Respectful, confident, no over-apologizing. Reaffirm the value, give them a clear
effective date and a way to ask questions. Under 160 words.
```
