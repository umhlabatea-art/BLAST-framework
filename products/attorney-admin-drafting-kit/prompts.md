# Solo Attorney Admin & Drafting Kit — Prompt Pack (Core)

> **Every output is an INTERNAL FIRST DRAFT requiring licensed-attorney review
> before it leaves the office.** These prompts do not provide legal advice. **Do
> not trust the model for legal conclusions, citations, or jurisdiction-specific
> rules — AI fabricates case citations, and lawyers have been sanctioned for
> filing them. Verify everything against primary sources.** Keep confidential
> client information out of public AI tools. See `DISCLAIMER.md`. Replace every
> `[BRACKET]`.

---

## 1. Client intake & screening

### 1.1 — Intake / screening email

```
Write a client intake email for a prospective client who inquired about [MATTER
TYPE]. Gather the facts I need to evaluate the matter — do NOT give legal advice
or opinions on the merits.

Information to collect: [PARTIES, DATES, DOCUMENTS, DEADLINES, JURISDICTION]
Firm intake notes: [FEE CONSULTATION? CONFLICTS PROCESS?]

Professional and welcoming. Include a clear statement that no attorney-client
relationship is formed until a signed engagement, and that the email is not legal
advice. Put the information requests in a numbered list.
```

## 2. Engagement / retainer letters

### 2.1 — Engagement-letter draft

```
Draft an engagement/retainer letter as an INTERNAL STARTING TEMPLATE for me to
review and adapt — not a final or jurisdiction-specific document.

Client: [NAME]  ·  Matter: [DESCRIPTION]  ·  Scope: [SCOPE]  ·  Out of scope: [X]
Fee arrangement: [HOURLY/FLAT/CONTINGENCY + DETAILS]
Jurisdiction: [STATE]

Include standard sections (scope, fees & billing, client responsibilities,
communication, confidentiality, termination). Insert [ATTORNEY REVIEW] markers at
every point requiring my judgment (fee terms, scope limits, jurisdiction-specific
disclosures, trust-accounting language). Do not assert jurisdiction-specific
rules — flag them for me to confirm.
```

---

## 3. Client status updates

### 3.1 — Matter status-update email

```
Write a status-update email to a client on their matter using ONLY the facts I
provide. No legal opinions or predictions of outcome beyond what I state.

Matter: [DESCRIPTION]
Recent developments (as I describe them): [FACTS]
Next steps & what I need from the client: [ITEMS]

Clear, reassuring, professional. Avoid guaranteeing outcomes. Under 200 words.
```

---

## 4. Demand-letter first drafts (internal)

### 4.1 — Demand-letter first draft

```
Produce an INTERNAL first draft of a demand letter for my review. This is a
starting point, not a filed or sent document.

Client (sender): [NAME]  ·  Recipient: [NAME]
Facts (as I provide them): [FACTS]
Relief sought: [AMOUNT/ACTION]  ·  Deadline: [DATE]
Tone: [FIRM/MEASURED]

Structure: parties, factual background, the demand, consequences of non-response,
deadline. **Do not cite statutes or case law** — leave [LEGAL BASIS — ATTORNEY TO
INSERT] placeholders where authority belongs. Flag every factual assertion I must
confirm.
```

## 5. Discovery / document summaries

### 5.1 — Summarize provided documents

```
Summarize the document(s) I paste below for internal case-organization purposes.
Summarize only what is in the text — do not infer legal significance or add
outside facts.

Documents:
"""
[PASTE — redact privileged/confidential identifiers as appropriate; prefer a
data-protected tool for sensitive material]
"""

Output: a neutral summary, key dates/parties/obligations as a table, and a list
of open questions the document raises for me to follow up. No legal conclusions.
```

## 6. Plain-English client explainers

### 6.1 — Plain-English explainer

```
Write a plain-English explainer for a client about [GENERAL LEGAL PROCESS/CONCEPT,
e.g. "what happens at a deposition"].

Keep it general and educational. Do NOT apply it to the client's specific facts or
give advice on their matter. Reading level ~grade 8. End with: "This is general
information, not legal advice about your matter — we'll discuss how it applies to
your case." I will review before sending.
```
